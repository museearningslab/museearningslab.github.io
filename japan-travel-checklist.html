Japan Trip Checklist: 15 Things to Prepare Before You Go

November 24, 2026

Fifteen practical preparations, in the order a Japanese local would check them — documents, money, connectivity, and the small things that cause big airport stress.

  1. Passport validity. Japan requires your passport to be valid for the duration of your stay, but your airline or a transit country may demand more. Check it months ahead — renewals are the slowest fix on this list.
  2. Visa requirements for your nationality. Many countries have visa-free short stays, but the permitted length and activities differ. Confirm your status on an official source, not a blog — including this one.
  3. Visit Japan Web registration. Japan's official online service lets you complete immigration and customs procedures in advance with a QR code. Registering before departure usually makes arrival noticeably smoother.
  4. Travel insurance. Medical care in Japan is high quality but you pay as a visitor, and costs for hospitalization add up fast. Make sure your policy covers medical expenses and check how claims work before you need them.
  5. Connectivity plan (eSIM or alternatives). Decide how you will get data before you land: travel eSIM, physical SIM, pocket Wi-Fi, or roaming. If you choose an eSIM, verify your phone supports it and is unlocked. Our guide explains the checks in detail: Best eSIM for Japan Travel: What to Check Before You Buy.
  6. A payment plan: cards plus some cash. Cards are widely accepted in cities, but small restaurants, shrines, rural areas, and transit IC card top-ups can still be cash territory. Carry both, and tell your bank you are traveling so transactions are not flagged.
  7. An IC card strategy. A rechargeable transit card (physical or the mobile version on compatible phones) covers trains, buses, and convenience-store payments. Decide in advance whether you will set up the mobile version at home or buy a physical card on arrival.
  8. Medication check. Some common overseas medicines are restricted or prohibited in Japan, and others require advance paperwork to bring in larger quantities. Check your specific medications against Japan's official rules well before departure, and carry prescriptions.
  9. Power adapters. Japan uses Type A plugs (two flat pins) at 100V. Most phone and laptop chargers handle the voltage fine; bring a plug adapter if your plugs differ, and leave high-wattage hair appliances at home unless they are dual-voltage.
  10. Hotel addresses in Japanese. Save each accommodation's address in Japanese (from the booking confirmation) in your phone. It is what taxi drivers, delivery services, and form fields actually need.
  11. A luggage plan. Japanese trains have limited luggage space and many stations have stairs. Pack lighter than you think, know your hotel's check-in time, and be aware that luggage forwarding services can send big bags to your next hotel — a very Japanese, very practical solution.
  12. Offline backups. Download offline maps of your destinations and a translation app's Japanese language pack while you still have home Wi-Fi. Connectivity problems always happen exactly when you need directions most.
  13. Copies of your documents. Photos or scans of your passport, insurance policy, and bookings — stored in the cloud and on your phone. If anything is lost, copies turn a disaster into an errand.
  14. Key reservations booked. Popular restaurants, attractions with timed entry, and long-distance trains in peak season can fill up. Book the few things your trip depends on; leave the rest flexible.
  15. Season-appropriate clothing — and comfortable shoes. Japan's summers are hot and humid, winters in the north are serious, and any season involves far more walking than most visitors expect. Shoes you have already broken in are worth more than anything on this list.

The night before departure

Charge everything, install and test your eSIM or confirm your connectivity alternative, screenshot your arrival-day plan (airport → transport → hotel), and put your passport, cards, and cash in the place you will actually check first. Then stop preparing — a trip to Japan rewards a little spontaneity too.
